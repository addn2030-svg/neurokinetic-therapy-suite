Level 2 Module 1: The 4 Functional Movement Subsystems

Movement occurs along continuous myofascial lines and force couples. In Level 2, we evaluate entire subsystem integrity and identify intra-subsystem and inter-subsystem compensations.

1. Posterior Oblique Subsystem (POS)

Anatomical Chain: Latissimus Dorsi → Thoracolumbar Fascia → Contralateral Gluteus Maximus.

Primary Function: Force transfer during propulsion/push-off in gait, rotational power generation, SI joint force closure.

Testing Protocol (Supine POS Integration Test):
• Patient supine. Right shoulder extended/internally rotated (arm pressed toward hip into table).
• Left leg extended with hip in slight external rotation (patient pushes left heel into table).
• MMT: Clinician attempts to flex the right arm while simultaneously lifting the left leg.
• Interpretation: Spongy give indicates POS failure. Challenge individual components (Right Lat vs. Left Glute Max).
2. Anterior Oblique Subsystem (AOS)

Anatomical Chain: External Oblique → Abdominal Fascia / Linea Alba → Contralateral Internal Oblique & Adductor Longus/Magnus.

Primary Function: Forward torso rotation, deceleration during throwing/swinging, pubic symphysis stabilization.

Testing Protocol (Supine AOS Diagonal Vector):
• Patient supine. Right shoulder flexed across chest toward left ASIS.
• Left hip flexed ($45^\circ$) and adducted toward midline.
• MMT: Clinician attempts to pry right shoulder back and push left knee into abduction.
• Interpretation: Common dyssynergia: Overactive Adductor Longus driving inhibition of contralateral External Oblique (Athletic Pubalgia).
3. Deep Longitudinal Subsystem (DLS)

Anatomical Chain: Erector Spinae → Sacrotuberous Ligament → Biceps Femoris → Peroneus Longus → Tibialis Anterior.

Primary Function: Ground reaction force transmission from foot contact through the pelvis to the cranium.

Testing Protocol: Prone straight-leg hip extension coupled with ipsilateral foot eversion/plantarflexion. Test for hamstring dominance over gluteals and sacrotuberous ligament tension.
4. Lateral Subsystem

Anatomical Chain: Gluteus Medius / Minimus → Tensor Fasciae Latae (TFL) → Contralateral Quadratus Lumborum (QL) & Adductors.

Primary Function: Frontal plane pelvic stabilization in single-limb stance.

Testing Protocol: Sidelying hip abduction against ipsilateral QL lateral trunk flexion. Common pattern: Hypertonic QL compensating for inhibited contralateral Gluteus Medius.

Level 2 Module 2: The Core Cylinder & Diaphragm Mechanics

2.1 The Diaphragm as the Master Core Regulator

The diaphragm is the muscular roof of the core cylinder. Chronic emotional stress, respiratory dysfunction, or postural collapse creates chronic diaphragmatic hypertonicity, leading to global downregulation of peripheral stabilizers.

Diaphragm Assessment Protocol (Inhalation vs. Exhalation Testing):

  • Step 1: Baseline Neuromuscular Test: Choose a strong indicator muscle (e.g., Anterior Deltoid or Psoas). Verify it is facilitated.
  • Step 2: Inhalation Phase Test: Patient inhales deeply and holds breath. Retest indicator muscle. If it breaks → Inhalation Diaphragm Facilitation.
  • Step 3: Exhalation Phase Test: Patient exhales completely and holds breath out. Retest indicator muscle. If it breaks → Exhalation Diaphragm Facilitation.
  • Step 4: Challenge & Release: Palpate subcostal margin / crus of the diaphragm. Perform 45s manual subcostal release, followed immediately by 3-5 exhalation core drawing-in activations.
2.2 The Intrinsic Core Cylinder Hierarchy

The Core Cylinder consists of 4 co-contracting boundaries:

  1. Roof: Respiratory Diaphragm
  2. Anterior/Lateral Walls: Transversus Abdominis (TVA) & Internal Obliques
  3. Posterior Wall: Lumbar Multifidi & Quadratus Lumborum
  4. Floor: Pelvic Floor (Levator Ani, Coccygeus, Pubococcygeus)
Testing Pelvic Floor via Kinetic Coupling:
• Pelvic floor cannot be tested directly in standard manual practice; evaluate via the Obturator Internus, Adductor Magnus, and Adductor Longus.
• Test prone hip external rotation at $90^\circ$ knee flexion (Obturator Internus). Challenge lower abdominal wall or perineal reflex to identify core-pelvic floor reactive pairs.

Level 2 Module 3: TMJ & Stomatognathic System

The jaw muscles act as the primary somatic default mechanism for whole-body overcompensation. Jaw clenching provides an emergency bracing mechanism when spinal stabilizers are dormant.

Muscle Primary Action Testing Vector Common Kinetic Chain Compensation
Temporalis Elevation & Retrusion Patient bites down on posterior molars; resistance against jaw opening. Compensates for Superficial Front Line (Deep Neck Flexors, TVA) & Superficial Back Line (Gluteals).
Masseter Elevation & Ipsilateral Translation Patient clenches jaw; clinician applies lateral shearing force. Compensates for Lateral Subsystem (Gluteus Medius, QL, Peroneals).
Medial Pterygoid Elevation & Contralateral Deviation Patient deviates jaw to opposite side against clinician resistance. Compensates for Contralateral Oblique slings and intrinsic pelvic floor. Associated with Tinnitus.
Lateral Pterygoid Depression, Protrusion & Contralateral Deviation Patient opens jaw and protrudes forward against chin resistance. Compensates for inhibited Deep Cervical Flexors and Upper Cervical Ligaments.
Digastric & Suprahyoids Jaw Depression / Hyoid Elevation Patient opens mouth wide against upward force under chin. Braces with Diaphragm & Pelvic floor during Valsalva compensation.
Clinical TMJ Protocol:

1. Find an inhibited muscle anywhere in the body (e.g., Left Gluteus Medius).
2. Have the client clench their jaw or deviate to the right → Retest Gluteus Medius.
3. If the glute immediately locks strong → The TMJ is the primary reactive driver!
4. Release the facilitated pterygoid/masseter (intra-oral or extra-oral) for 45s → Activate the glute.

Level 2 Module 4: Perturbation & Joint Compression/Decompression

4.1 Perturbation / Balance Assessment

Perturbation tests evaluate the central nervous system's automatic anticipatory postural adjustments (APAs) in response to unexpected mechanical displacement.

  • Anteroposterior (A/P) Perturbation: Patient standing or seated tall. Clinician delivers sudden, gentle sternal push (posterior perturbation) or thoracic pull (anterior perturbation). Observe whether trunk corrects via intrinsic core vs. superficial neck/hip gripping.
  • Rotational / Oblique Perturbation: Deliver gentle rotational displacement at shoulders/pelvis. Test for contralateral oblique sling engagement.
4.2 Joint Compression vs. Decompression Protocols

Joint capsules are dense with Ruffini and Pacinian mechanoreceptors that reflexively modulate muscular tone:

Joint Compression Testing (e.g., Hip):
• Patient supine. Flex hip to $90^\circ$.
• Apply axial compressive force down the shaft of the femur into the acetabulum for 2 seconds.
• Retest a key indicator muscle (e.g., Psoas or Gluteus Medius).
• If the muscle weakens: Pathological capsule compression sensitivity (Hypertonic deep rotators).
Joint Decompression / Distraction Testing:
• Apply gentle axial long-axis traction to the joint (hip, ankle, or glenohumeral joint).
• Retest indicator muscle.
• If the muscle weakens: Ligamentous instability or capsular laxity driving protective muscular splinting.

Level 3 Module 5: Advanced Multi-Phasic Gait Testing

Level 3 evaluates the dynamic coordination of reciprocal gait phases. Every phase of human gait requires an exact coupling of ipsilateral and contralateral limbs.

GAIT PHASE NEUROLOGICAL COUPLING MATRIX: ======================================================================================== Phase 1: Heel Strike (Initial Contact) --> Ankle Dorsiflexion + Contralat Arm Extension Phase 2: Loading Response / Mid-Stance --> Gluteus Medius + Contralat Quadratus Lumborum Phase 3: Terminal Stance / Push-Off --> Gluteus Max + Contralat Latissimus Dorsi (POS) Phase 4: Swing Phase --> Iliopsoas + Contralat Shoulder Extension ========================================================================================
Hands-On Gait Testing Vectors (Supine & Standing):

1. Reciprocal Humeral-Femoral Gait Vector (Stance/Swing Coupling):

• Patient supine. Instruct: Flex Right Humerus ($90^\circ$) while simultaneously extending Left Hip (pressing heel down into table).
• MMT: Clinician attempts to push the right arm into extension while lifting the left leg into flexion.
• Assess the opposite pair: Left Humerus Flexion + Right Hip Extension.
• Diagnostic Yield: Uncovers cross-body kinetic sling breakdown undetectable during isolated single-joint testing.

2. Propulsion Vector (Toe-Off):

• Prone. Patient performs terminal knee extension, ankle plantarflexion/inversion, and hip extension.
• Test against resisted contralateral latissimus extension. Identifies loss of push-off force transmission.

Level 3 Module 6: Multi-Vector Scar Protocol

Surgical and traumatic scars create permanent sensory disruption. In Level 3, we test scars in 8 distinct directional planes and evaluate complex Scar-to-Scar and Scar-to-Ligament relationships.

The 8-Vector Scar Assessment Protocol:

1. Find an inhibited muscle (e.g., Transversus Abdominis or Gluteus Maximus).
2. Test the scar in specific directional shears while retesting the weak muscle:

Corrective Treatment: Apply 45–60 seconds of manual cross-fiber mobilization precisely along the vector that restored muscle strength, followed immediately by 3-5 reps of isolated activation for the inhibited muscle.
Special Scar Topologies:
  • Umbilicus / Navel Scar: The original birth scar. Affects the visceral fascia (urachus/median umbilical ligament) and frequently drives deep lumbar/pelvic floor inhibition.
  • Episiotomy / Perineal Scars: Primary drivers of coccygeus, piriformis, and obturator internus hypertonicity.
  • Scar-to-Scar Reactivity: A C-section scar can be reactive to an older Appendectomy scar or laparoscopic portal. Test TL on Scar A while challenging Scar B.

Level 3 Module 7: Advanced Ligamentous Interlinks

Ligaments possess a high concentration of mechanoreceptors (Ruffini, Pacinian, and Type IV free nerve endings). When damaged or lax, the Ligamento-Muscular Reflex triggers protective muscular hypertonicity across surrounding joints while inhibiting dynamic stabilizers.

Comprehensive Ligament Testing Inventory:

1. Pelvic & Lumbar Ligaments:

  • Sacrotuberous Ligament: Tested with ischial tuberosity to sacral shear. Compensates for Biceps Femoris / Glute Max.
  • Sacrospinous Ligament: Challenged via deep palpation medial to ischial spine. Linked to Pudendal nerve irritation.
  • Iliolumbar Ligament: Challenged via L5 transverse process to iliac crest glide. Compensates for QL / Psoas.
  • Dorsal Sacroiliac Ligaments: Assessed via posterior SIJ seam distraction.

2. Ankle & Foot Ligaments:

  • Anterior Talofibular (ATFL): Plantarflexion + Inversion stress. Drives Peroneus & Glute Med inhibition.
  • Calcaneofibular (CFL): Pure Inversion stress in neutral dorsiflexion.
  • Deltoid Ligament Complex: Eversion stress. Drives Tibialis Posterior & Core inhibition.
  • Bifurcate Ligament: Palpated in sinus tarsi; key in chronic lateral foot pain.
  • Spring Ligament: Plantar calcaneonavicular stress. Drives medial longitudinal arch collapse.
Ligament-to-Ligament Interlink Protocol:
• Ligaments can compensate for other ligaments! (e.g., Chronic ATFL sprain compensated by ipsilateral Sacrotuberous ligament).
• Test: Challenge Ligament A (stress ATFL) → Test indicator → Weak.
• Challenge: Touch/Stress Ligament B (Sacrotuberous) → Retest indicator → Locks Strong!
• Correction: Friction release the compensating ligament (30s) → Proprioceptively stimulate the lax ligament (tap/vibrate for 15s).

Level 3 Module 8: Extraocular Muscles & Cranio-Cervical Reflexes

8.1 Extraocular Muscle Testing (Oculo-Cervical Reflex)

The extraocular muscles have direct neural connections through the tectospinal and vestibulospinal tracts to the suboccipital complex (Rectus Capitis Posterior Major/Minor, Obliquus Capitis Superior/Inferior).

Cranial Nerve Extraocular Muscle Visual Gaze Vector Kinetic Chain Reflex Connection
CN III (Oculomotor) Superior Rectus Look Up & Out Posterior chain extension tone (Suboccipitals, Erectors).
CN III (Oculomotor) Inferior Rectus Look Down & Out Anterior chain flexion tone (Deep neck flexors, Abdominals).
CN III (Oculomotor) Medial Rectus Look Medially (Inward) Adductor line / Deep Front Line activation.
CN III (Oculomotor) Inferior Oblique Look Up & In Contralateral cervical rotation & lateral sling.
CN IV (Trochlear) Superior Oblique Look Down & In Ipsilateral vestibular balance & core stabilization.
CN VI (Abducens) Lateral Rectus Look Laterally (Outward) Lateral subsystem / Gluteus Medius / SCM coupling.
Clinical Testing Protocol:
1. Identify an inhibited muscle (e.g., Deep Cervical Flexors or Gluteus Medius).
2. Have patient hold maximum gaze in a specific quadrant (e.g., Eyes hard Up and to the Right).
3. Retest the inhibited muscle while gaze is held.
4. If it immediately locks strong → The Extraocular Muscle is the reactive driver!
5. Correct by performing eye-tracking smooth pursuit/saccade drills coupled with neck stabilizer activation.
8.2 Hyoid Musculature & Deglutition (Swallowing) Reflex

The hyoid bone acts as a free-floating suspension bridge connecting the cranium, mandible, cervical spine, and thorax.

  • Suprahyoids (Mylohyoid, Geniohyoid, Stylohyoid, Digastric): Tested via resisted hyoid depression / jaw opening. Hypertonicity leads to anterior neck tension and thoracic outlet compression.
  • Infrahyoids (Sternohyoid, Omohyoid, Sternothyroid, Thyrohyoid): Tested via resisted swallowing or cervical extension. Linked directly to fascial tension in the clavicle and first rib.

Reverse Therapy Localization (Reverse TL) Technique:

When to use: When you have a client with severe pain in a hypertonic/facilitated tissue, but cannot easily find what is inhibited.

  1. Touch the painful, hypertonic tissue (Driver).
  2. Scan through potential inhibited muscles across the kinetic chain (e.g., Glute Max, TVA, Lower Trap).
  3. When you test a muscle that is normally strong, but breaks weak when touching the driver, you have found the inhibited receiver!
  4. Release the painful driver → Activate the newly uncovered inhibited muscle.
