1. Interface Overview: Joint & Muscle Selection System

🎯 Purpose of the Hierarchical Interface

The joint-first, muscle-second dropdown system mirrors the clinical reasoning process in NeuroKinetic Therapy:

  • Anatomical Logic: Joints are primary movement centers; muscles are the motors
  • Patient-Centered: Patients report by region ("shoulder hurts"), not specific muscle
  • Kinetic Chain Thinking: Test muscles that cross or stabilize the same joint
  • Efficiency: Organizes 600+ muscles into manageable joint categories

How the Interface Works

Step 1: Select Joint

Start where patient reports symptoms or functional testing reveals dysfunction

Step 2: Select Muscle

Muscles grouped by function for systematic testing

Design Feature Clinical Benefit Example
Functional Grouping Test agonist-synergist relationships Hip Extensors: Glute Max, Hamstrings, Adductor Magnus
Cross-Joint Muscles Identify multi-joint compensation Rectus Femoris in Hip Flexors AND Knee Extensors
Bilateral Options Test left vs. right asymmetry Right Psoas vs. Left Psoas in pelvic rotation

Phase 1: Initial Patient Intake

  • "When did this start? Any injury history?"

  • Single-Leg Stance: Hip drop, knee valgus
  • Squat: Lumbar flexion, heel lift

Upper Body Screens

  • Overhead Reach: Scapular winging
  • Cervical Rotation: Asymmetry
  • Push-Up: Scapular winging
Example: Patient limps with shortened right stance phase

  3. Apply 2-second isometric ramp force
  4. Classify as FACILITATED (strong) or INHIBITED (weak)
⚠️ Critical: If muscle tests STRONG, it's not the problem! Select different muscle.
Step 4

Identify Potential Drivers

3. Testing Methodology: Hands-On Muscle Testing Techniques

Universal NKT Testing Principles

  • ⚡ Isometric Contraction: Patient matches force with NO joint movement
  • 📏 Shortened Position: Start in inner-mid range to isolate active force
  • ⏱️ 2-Second Ramp: Smooth 0% → 50% → 100% force application
  • 🎯 Vector Precision: Force aligns with muscle's exact line of pull
  • 🔒 Binary Classification: Facilitated (crisp lock) vs. Inhibited (spongy give)
  • 💪 10-15 lbs Force: Testing motor control, not strength

4. Interpretation Framework: Analyzing Test Results

Understanding Compensation Patterns

Pattern Type Mechanism Clinical Example
Synergistic Dominance Synergist takes over for weak prime mover Hamstrings dominate for inhibited Glute Max → hamstring strains
Reciprocal Inhibition Tight antagonist inhibits agonist Tight Psoas inhibits Glute Max → anterior pelvic tilt
Arthrokinematic Dysfunction Joint restriction causes muscle inhibition Restricted SIJ inhibits ipsilateral Glute Max
Ligamento-Muscular Reflex Ligament injury inhibits distant muscle Ankle sprain (ATFL) inhibits ipsilateral Glute Max

Decision Matrix: What the Tests Tell You

Scenario 1: Local Muscle Pair Found

Test Results:
  • Right Gluteus Maximus: INHIBITED (weak)
  • TL to Right Hamstrings → Glute Max becomes STRONG
Interpretation: Synergistic dominance pattern. Hamstrings compensating for weak glute.
Treatment: Release hamstrings → Activate glute max

Scenario 2: Antagonist Pattern

Test Results:
  • Right Gluteus Maximus: INHIBITED
  • TL to Right Psoas → Glute Max becomes STRONG
Interpretation: Reciprocal inhibition. Tight hip flexor inhibiting hip extensor.
Treatment: Release psoas → Activate glute max

Scenario 3: No Local Driver Found

Test Results:
  • Right Gluteus Maximus: INHIBITED
  • TL to all local muscles (hamstrings, psoas, erector spinae) → Still WEAK
  • Patient has C-section scar
  • TL to C-section scar (pulling superior) → Glute Max becomes STRONG
Interpretation: Scar tissue creating fascial restriction and neurological inhibition.
Treatment: Scar tissue release → Activate glute max

5. Treatment Application: From Assessment to Correction

The 6-Step NKT Treatment Protocol

Step 1

Confirm Baseline Inhibition

Test suspected muscle - must be INHIBITED (weak/spongy)

Step 2

Therapy Localization

Apply sensory stimulation to hypothesized driver (touch or rub 2-3 seconds)

Step 3

Immediate Retest

Retest inhibited muscle within 2 seconds. If STRONG → reactive pair confirmed!

Step 4

Release Facilitated Driver

Techniques (choose one):

  • Ischemic Compression: Sustained pressure on trigger point 30-60 seconds
  • Pin & Stretch: Pin muscle, move joint through ROM
  • IASTM: Instrument-assisted soft tissue mobilization
  • Foam Rolling: Patient self-release with sustained pressure

Duration: 30-60 seconds to modulate muscle spindle afferents

Step 5

Activate Inhibited Receiver

⚠️ CRITICAL: Must activate within 30-60 second neuroplastic window after release!

Activation Protocol:

  • 3-5 repetitions
  • 5-second isometric hold each rep
  • 50-70% effort (moderate intensity)
  • Isolated contraction (no substitution)
Example - Gluteus Maximus Activation:
Position: Prone, knee flexed 90°
Cue: "Squeeze your glute and lift heel toward ceiling. Hold 5 seconds."
Repeat 3-5 times with 2-second rest between reps
Step 6

Retest & Prescribe Homework

Immediate Retest: Receiver must now test STRONG in isolation

Home Exercise Prescription:

🚨 NON-NEGOTIABLE SEQUENCE:
1. Release Driver FIRST
2. Activate Receiver SECOND

Never reverse this order!
  • Frequency: 2-3 times daily
  • Duration: 2-4 weeks for synaptic consolidation
  • Tools: Foam roller, lacrosse ball, or manual pressure for release

Treatment Techniques by Muscle

Facilitated Driver Release Technique Patient Position Duration

Psoas Deep abdominal release Supine, hip/knee flexed, lateral to rectus 30-60 sec
Upper Trapezius Ischemic compression Seated or prone 30-60 sec
TFL Pin & stretch or foam roll Side-lying 30-60 sec
C-section Scar Scar mobilization (8 vectors) Supine 30 sec per vector

7. Clinical Decision Trees: Common Presentations

CASE STUDY 1: Low Back Pain

Presentation: 35-year-old office worker, chronic low back pain, worse with sitting

Interface Navigation Path:

  1. Joint Selection: Lumbar Spine
  2. Functional Test: Observe sitting posture → anterior pelvic tilt, lumbar lordosis
  3. Suspected Inhibition: Lumbar Spine → Spinal Extensors → Multifidus
  4. Test Result: Multifidus INHIBITED
  5. Test Drivers:
    • Hip Joint → Hip Flexors → Psoas (TL) → Multifidus STRONG ✓
  6. Treatment: Release psoas → Activate multifidus
  7. Additional Testing: Hip Joint → Hip Extensors → Gluteus Maximus (often also inhibited)

Outcome: Improved sitting tolerance, reduced lordosis, pain decreased 70%

CASE STUDY 2: Shoulder Impingement

Presentation: 42-year-old swimmer, anterior shoulder pain with overhead reach

Interface Navigation Path:

  1. Joint Selection: Shoulder Complex
  2. Functional Test: Overhead reach → scapular winging, rib flare
  3. Suspected Inhibition: Shoulder Complex → Scapular Depressors → Lower Trapezius
  4. Test Result: Lower Trapezius INHIBITED
  5. Test Drivers:
    • Shoulder Complex → Scapular Elevators → Upper Trapezius (TL) → Lower Trap STRONG ✓
  6. Treatment: Release upper trap → Activate lower trap
  7. Additional Testing: Shoulder Complex → Scapular Protractors → Pectoralis Minor (often also facilitated)

Outcome: Overhead ROM improved, scapular mechanics normalized, pain-free swimming

CASE STUDY 3: Knee Pain (IT Band Syndrome)

Presentation: 28-year-old runner, lateral knee pain at 2 miles

Interface Navigation Path:

  1. Joint Selection: Hip Joint (NOT Knee Joint - think proximal!)
  2. Functional Test: Single-leg stance → Trendelenburg, knee valgus
  3. Suspected Inhibition: Hip Joint → Hip Abductors → Gluteus Medius
  4. Test Result: Gluteus Medius INHIBITED
  5. Test Drivers:
    • Hip Joint → Hip Flexors → TFL (TL) → Glute Med STRONG ✓
  6. Treatment: Release TFL → Activate glute medius
  7. Descending Chain: Also test Ankle/Foot → Ankle Invertors → Tibialis Posterior

Outcome: Running distance increased to 5+ miles pain-free, improved hip stability

CASE STUDY 4: Plantar Fasciitis

Presentation: 50-year-old teacher, heel pain first steps in morning

Interface Navigation Path:

  1. Joint Selection: Ankle/Foot Complex
  2. Functional Test: Observe gait → foot pronation, collapsed arch
  3. Suspected Inhibition: Ankle/Foot → Ankle Invertors → Tibialis Posterior
  4. Test Result: Tibialis Posterior INHIBITED
  5. Test Drivers (Local):
    • Ankle/Foot → Ankle Evertors → Peroneals (TL) → Tib Post still WEAK ✗
  6. Ascending Chain Testing:
    • Hip Joint → Hip Extensors → Gluteus Maximus (TL) → Tib Post STRONG ✓
  7. Treatment: Release glute max → Activate tibialis posterior

Outcome: Morning pain resolved, arch support improved, returned to standing work

8. Safety Considerations & Contraindications

🚨 ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATIONS - DO NOT TEST/TREAT

  • Cauda Equina Syndrome: Saddle anesthesia, bowel/bladder incontinence, bilateral leg weakness
  • Acute Fracture: Unhealed bone fracture in test region
  • Active Infection: Fever with spinal tenderness, systemic infection
  • Malignancy: Known bone metastases, unexplained weight loss with pain
  • Acute DVT: Deep vein thrombosis, risk of embolism
  • Severe Osteoporosis: T-score < -3.0, high fracture risk

⚠️ RELATIVE PRECAUTIONS - Modify Testing

  • Acute Surgical Scars (<6-8 weeks): Use light touch only, no aggressive friction
  • Pregnancy: Avoid prone positioning after 20 weeks, modify psoas testing
  • Acute Inflammation: Reduce force, avoid aggravating inflamed tissues
  • Hypermobility Syndromes: Calibrate force carefully, focus on stability
  • Neurological Conditions: MS, Parkinson's - interpret results cautiously

Joint-Specific Safety Guidelines

Joint/Region Precautions Modifications
Cervical Spine Vertebral artery insufficiency, cervical instability Avoid end-range rotation, reduce force to 5-8 lbs
Lumbar Spine Acute disc herniation, spondylolisthesis Avoid flexion testing if acute, test in neutral
Shoulder Complex Recent dislocation, rotator cuff tear Test in pain-free range, avoid end-range positions
Hip Joint Hip replacement (<12 weeks), labral tear Respect hip precautions, avoid combined motions
Knee Joint ACL reconstruction, meniscus repair Avoid valgus stress, test in protected range

When to Refer Out

Red Flags Requiring Medical Referral:

  • Progressive Neurological Deficits: Worsening weakness, numbness, or loss of reflexes
  • Night Pain: Severe pain that wakes patient, not relieved by position changes
  • Constitutional Symptoms: Unexplained fever, chills, weight loss
  • Trauma with Deformity: Visible bone deformity, severe swelling
  • No Response to Treatment: No improvement after 4-6 sessions

Conditions Requiring Co-Management:

  • Complex Regional Pain Syndrome: Work with pain management specialist
  • Chronic Pain Syndrome: Coordinate with psychologist/psychiatrist
  • Autoimmune Conditions: Communicate with rheumatologist
  • Post-Surgical: Clear with surgeon before aggressive treatment

Patient Education & Informed Consent

Before Testing, Explain:

  • Purpose of muscle testing (assessing motor control, not strength)
  • What they'll feel (moderate pressure, should not be painful)
  • Expected outcomes (improved movement, reduced pain)
  • Potential soreness (mild DOMS for 24-48 hours)

During Treatment, Monitor:

  • Pain levels (should not exceed 4/10 during treatment)
  • Autonomic responses (sweating, nausea, dizziness)
  • Patient tolerance (adjust force/duration as needed)

Post-Treatment Instructions:

  • Expect mild soreness (normal adaptation)
  • Perform HEP as prescribed (critical for reprogramming)
  • Avoid aggravating activities for 24-48 hours
  • Report any unusual symptoms immediately
